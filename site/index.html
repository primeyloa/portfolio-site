About Me

Hello! I'm Benedict — a computer science student who fell in love with the why and how behind the models and frameworks everyone else just uses. That curiosity has taken me from debugging dependency conflicts in LoRA fine-tuning pipelines to publishing research on bias in content moderation systems.

My recent work sits at the intersection of machine learning and real impact: fine-tuning MedGemma to recognise tropical skin diseases on dark skin tones — a group dermatology AI largely underserves — and packaging Twi language data so other researchers can build for a low-resource Ghanaian language.

When I'm not training models, I lead backend engineering teams, ship payment-integrated platforms, and run a student community that helps young builders turn ideas into real projects.

Crisis-Driven Bias in Social Media Flagging: Quantifying Topic Sensitivity in Content Moderation Classifiers

Preprint · 2026 · DOI 10.13140/RG.2.2.24694.36165

First-author preprint investigating how content-moderation classifiers become more sensitive to certain topics during crisis periods, and how that heightened sensitivity turns into flagging bias. Contributes methods for measuring topic sensitivity at the intersection of NLP and algorithmic fairness.

Data Intelligence & Swarm Analytics (DISAL) · 2025–2026 · Applied research

Research conducted during my internship at DISAL: fine-tuning MedGemma 4B with LoRA for tropical skin diseases prevalent in Africa, with dataset curation, image-quality enhancement and systematic evaluation — deployed optimised for dark (Fitzpatrick V–VI) skin tones to reduce prediction bias.
